# Strategic Advisor Framework

## Core Question

**승리할 전략적 포지션은?** (What strategic position wins?)

## Score: Strategic Position (0-10)

This framework evaluates the idea's strategic viability through mental models, competitive positioning, and long-term defensibility.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Competitive Moat Potential (0-10)

**What to assess:** Can you build defensible advantages over time?

| Score | Moat Level | Criteria |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 0-2 | No moat | Easily cloned, commodity features, no switching costs |
| 3-4 | Weak moat | Minor UX advantages, low switching costs |
| 5-6 | Moderate moat | Some data advantage, moderate switching costs, brand potential |
| 7-8 | Strong moat | Network effects, significant data moat, high switching costs |
| 9-10 | Deep moat | Multiple reinforcing moats, winner-take-most dynamics |

**Moat types to evaluate:**
- **Network effects**: Does the product get better with more users?
- **Data moat**: Does usage generate proprietary data advantage?
- **Switching costs**: How painful is it for users to leave?
- **Brand/trust**: Can you build category authority?
- **Scale economies**: Do costs decrease meaningfully at scale?
- **Embedding**: Does the product become infrastructure?

### 2. Differentiation Clarity (0-10)

**What to assess:** Is there a clear, communicable reason to choose this over alternatives?

| Score | Clarity Level | Criteria |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 0-2 | Undifferentiated | "Same thing but slightly better" — no clear angle |
| 3-4 | Weak differentiation | Minor feature differences, hard to communicate |
| 5-6 | Moderate | Clear angle but not compelling enough to switch |
| 7-8 | Strong | Obvious reason to choose, easy to communicate |
| 9-10 | Category-defining | Creates a new category or redefines existing one |

**Differentiation archetypes:**
- **10x better at one thing** (not 10% better at everything)
- **Radically simpler** (remove 80% of features competitors have)
- **Niche-first** (purpose-built for underserved segment)
- **Price disruptor** (same value at fraction of cost)
- **New paradigm** (AI-native, mobile-first where others aren't)

### 3. Strategic Mental Models (0-10)

**What to assess:** Does the idea align with proven strategic patterns?

Apply these mental models and score alignment:

**Blue Ocean vs Red Ocean:**
- Red ocean (0-3): Competing on same dimensions as incumbents
- Blue ocean (7-10): Creating uncontested market space

**Aggregation Theory:**
- Does it aggregate supply or demand? (Higher score if yes)
- Can it become the interface between suppliers and consumers?

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
- Is the "job" clearly defined?
- Is the current "hiring" process broken enough to switch?

**Crossing the Chasm:**
- Is there a clear beachhead segment?
- Can you dominate a niche before expanding?

**Disruption Theory:**
- Does it start simpler/cheaper and improve? (Classic disruption)
- Or does it serve overshot customers? (New market disruption)

### 4. Long-term Strategic Position (0-10)

**What to assess:** Where does this end up in 3-5 years?

| Score | Position Level | Criteria |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| 0-2 | Dead end | No expansion path, single feature, easily obsoleted |
| 3-4 | Limited | Small addressable expansion, narrow niche forever |
| 5-6 | Growth path | Clear v2/v3 expansion, adjacent markets possible |
| 7-8 | Platform potential | Can become platform or ecosystem, multiple revenue streams |
| 9-10 | Category leader | Can define and lead a category, compounding advantages |

**Strategic expansion assessment:**
- What are natural product extensions?
- Can you move upmarket over time?
- Is there a platform play (marketplace, ecosystem)?
- Does the business compound (data, network, brand)?

## Scoring Formula

```
Strategic Position = (Moat × 30%) + (Differentiation × 30%) + (Mental Models × 20%) + (Long-term × 20%)
```

## Common Pitfalls

- **Feature-level thinking**: Strategy is about positioning, not features
- **Moat fantasy**: Most solo products don't have network effects — be honest
- **Differentiation by addition**: Adding features ≠ differentiation. Often it's about removing
- **Ignoring incumbents' response**: If your idea works, what will competitors do?
- **Over-rotating on long-term**: You need to survive short-term first

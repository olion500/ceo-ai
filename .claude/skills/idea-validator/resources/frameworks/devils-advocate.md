# Devil's Advocate Framework

## Core Question

**내가 뭘 틀리고 있는가?** (What am I getting wrong?)

## Score: Intellectual Honesty (0-10)

This framework stress-tests assumptions, detects cognitive biases, and forces confrontation with uncomfortable truths. Higher score = fewer unexamined assumptions.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Assumption Audit (0-10)

**What to assess:** How many critical assumptions are unvalidated?

**Core assumptions to challenge:**

| Assumption | Evidence? | Confidence | If Wrong? |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| "People have this problem" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "They'll pay to solve it" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "My solution is better" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "I can build this" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "I can reach customers" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "Market timing is right" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "This hasn't been tried" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |
| "Revenue model will work" | [Y/N] | [H/M/L] | [Impact] |

**Scoring:**
- 0-2: 5+ critical assumptions unvalidated, high confidence without evidence
- 3-4: 3-4 unvalidated assumptions, some evidence gathered
- 5-6: 2-3 unvalidated assumptions, most have partial evidence
- 7-8: 1 unvalidated assumption, strong evidence for most claims
- 9-10: All critical assumptions validated with evidence

### 2. Cognitive Bias Detection (0-10)

**What to assess:** Which biases are distorting the founder's judgment?

**Bias checklist:**

| Bias | Description | Red Flag Signs | Present? |
|------|------------|---------------|---------|
| **Confirmation bias** | Seeking only supporting evidence | Only positive feedback cited | [Y/N] |
| **Survivorship bias** | Looking only at successes | "Look at [famous product]!" | [Y/N] |
| **Dunning-Kruger** | Overestimating ability in unfamiliar domain | "How hard can it be?" | [Y/N] |
| **Sunk cost** | Continuing because of past investment | "We've already spent X" | [Y/N] |
| **Anchoring** | Fixated on first number/idea | Won't consider alternatives | [Y/N] |
| **Optimism bias** | Systematic overestimation of success | "This will definitely work" | [Y/N] |
| **IKEA effect** | Overvaluing own creation | "My solution is clearly better" | [Y/N] |
| **Bandwagon** | Following trends without analysis | "AI is hot, so AI + X = success" | [Y/N] |

**Scoring:**
- 0-2: 5+ biases actively distorting judgment, founder defensive about feedback
- 3-4: 3-4 biases present, some self-awareness
- 5-6: 2 biases present, actively working to counter them
- 7-8: 1 minor bias, high self-awareness, seeking disconfirming evidence
- 9-10: Actively debunking own assumptions, open to being wrong

### 3. Counter-Argument Strength (0-10)

**What to assess:** How strong are the arguments AGAINST this idea?

**Devil's advocate questions:**

1. **"Why hasn't someone already done this?"**
   - If they have: Why will you win?
   - If they haven't: Why not? (No market? Too hard? Bad timing?)

2. **"What if you get 0 customers in 3 months?"**
   - What's your honest reaction? Pivot? Double down? Quit?
   - Have you defined failure criteria?

3. **"What would make you abandon this idea?"**
   - If nothing: That's a bias, not conviction
   - Clear kill criteria = intellectual honesty

4. **"Who specifically would NOT buy this and why?"**
   - If you can't name groups: You don't understand the market
   - Anti-personas reveal true positioning

5. **"What's the boring explanation for why competitors exist?"**
   - They're not all stupid — they may see something you don't

**Scoring:**
- 0-2: Cannot articulate strong counter-arguments (tunnel vision)
- 3-4: Weak counter-arguments, easily dismissed
- 5-6: Moderate counter-arguments acknowledged, partial responses
- 7-8: Strong counter-arguments with thoughtful rebuttals
- 9-10: Can argue against own idea as effectively as for it

## Scoring Formula

```
Intellectual Honesty = (Assumptions × 40%) + (Bias Detection × 30%) + (Counter-Arguments × 30%)
```

## Common Pitfalls

- **Treating this framework as negativity**: It's not about being negative — it's about being prepared
- **Dismissing uncomfortable truths**: The points that make you defensive are often the most important
- **Confusing conviction with evidence**: Strong belief ≠ strong evidence
- **Not defining failure criteria**: If you can't define failure, you can't recognize it

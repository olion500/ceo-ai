# Platformer Success Story

## The Story

### Problem Discovery

Casey Newton was the longtime Silicon Valley editor of The Verge, writing the daily newsletter "The Interface" which grew to 20,000 subscribers. He identified that tech platform coverage at the intersection of technology and democracy deserved dedicated, independent focus.

**Key insight:** Major tech platforms' impact on democracy needed deep, consistent coverage that traditional media couldn't always provide.

### MVP Development

**Timeline:** October 2020

**Initial Features:**

- Daily newsletter on tech platforms and democracy
- Investigative journalism focus
- Paid subscription model from day one

**Tech Stack:**

- Substack (initial platform)
- Later migrated to custom platform

**Development Process:**

- Brought existing 20,000 subscriber list from The Interface
- Launched with established reputation
- Immediate monetization

### Launch Strategy

**Platform:** Substack (later independent)

**Date:** October 2020

**Pre-launch:**

- Built audience at The Verge with The Interface newsletter
- Established reputation as platform beat journalist

**Launch Results:**

- Started with 24,000 subscribers from existing list
- 12 months later: 49,604 free subscribers
- Year 1 growth exceeded expectations

### Growth Journey

#### Phase 1: Launch with Existing Audience (October 2020)

- 24,000 subscribers from The Interface
- Immediate paid tier launch
- Social media (Twitter) was primary growth driver

#### Phase 2: Rapid Growth via Journalism (Year 1-2)

- Breaking major stories drove significant growth
- One major story: 400,000 readers, 50 new paid subscribers ($5,000 value)
- Grew to 49,604 free subscribers in first year

#### Phase 3: Team Expansion (Year 3)

- Hired managing editor Zoë Schiffer (full-time)
- Added part-time contributor Lindsey Choo
- Revenue: ~$775,000 before fees

#### Phase 4: Platform Migration & Advertising

- Left Substack due to content moderation concerns (Nazi monetization)
- Migrated to independent platform
- Began experimenting with ads to fund additional hires
- Revenue up 11% year over year despite migration

### Key Decisions

1. **Leave The Verge for independent newsletter**
   - **Why:** Creative control, direct relationship with readers
   - **Result:** Built $775K+ business

2. **Paid tier from day one**
   - **Why:** Substack suggested 10% conversion target
   - **Result:** ~5% conversion (below target but sustainable)

3. **Leave Substack**
   - **Why:** Content moderation concerns (allowing Nazis to monetize)
   - **Result:** Maintained audience, revenue grew 11%

4. **Add advertising**
   - **Why:** Subscription-only limits hiring ability
   - **Result:** Enables team expansion (one ad per weekly free edition)

### Pivots & Changes

- **Substack → Independent platform:** Left due to content moderation issues
- **Subscription-only → Adding ads:** To fund additional journalism positions

### Marketing Channels

**Primary Channel (2020):** Twitter

> "The only way a Substack grows is through tweets" - Newton (2021)

**Evolution (2023):** Twitter's decline

- Cross-posting across multiple platforms
- Diminished returns compared to 2020-2021

**Breaking Stories:** Major investigations drive growth

- One story: 400,000 readers → 50 paid subscribers → $5,000 immediate value

**Failed Attempts:**

- Reliance on Twitter becoming less effective

### Revenue Model

**Pricing:** $10/month or $100/year

**Revenue Breakdown:**

- ~155,000 total subscribers
- ~95% free (147,000)
- ~5% paid (~7,750)
- Revenue: ~$775,000/year (before Substack 10% + Stripe fees)

**Conversion Rate:** ~5% (below Substack's suggested 10%)

### Challenges & Solutions

1. **Challenge:** Lower than expected conversion rate
   - **Solution:** Focus on quality journalism over conversion optimization
   - **Result:** Sustainable ~5% conversion

2. **Challenge:** Subscription limits hiring
   - **Solution:** Adding advertising revenue
   - **Result:** Enables expanding team

3. **Challenge:** Twitter's decline as growth channel
   - **Solution:** Cross-posting, breaking exclusive stories
   - **Result:** Continued growth despite platform changes

## All Tactics & Strategies Used

### Tactic 1: Leverage Existing Audience

- **What They Did:** Brought 20,000 subscribers from The Interface
- **How They Implemented:** Email list migration
- **Context:** Established journalist with existing reputation
- **Results Achieved:** 24,000 day-one subscribers
- **Time Investment:** Years at The Verge
- **Cost:** Reputation capital

### Tactic 2: Breaking Exclusive Stories

- **What They Did:** Investigative journalism on tech platforms
- **How They Implemented:** Deep reporting on platform companies
- **Context:** Unique value proposition
- **Results Achieved:** One story: 400K readers, 50 paid subs, $5K value
- **Time Investment:** Significant investigation time
- **Cost:** Journalism resources

### Tactic 3: Twitter-First Distribution (2020-2021)

- **What They Did:** Heavily used Twitter for content distribution
- **How They Implemented:** Tweets driving to newsletter
- **Context:** "The only way a Substack grows is through tweets"
- **Results Achieved:** Primary growth driver in Year 1
- **Time Investment:** Daily posting
- **Cost:** $0

### Tactic 4: Platform Migration for Values

- **What They Did:** Left Substack over content moderation concerns
- **How They Implemented:** Built independent platform
- **Context:** Nazis monetizing on Substack
- **Results Achieved:** Revenue grew 11% YoY despite migration
- **Time Investment:** Significant migration effort
- **Cost:** Development costs

## Founder's Lessons & Reflections

### What They Say Worked

- Breaking exclusive stories (400K readers from one story)
- Existing audience from The Verge
- Quality journalism over conversion optimization

### What They Say Didn't Work

> "The only way a Substack grows is through tweets" (2021) has become outdated - Twitter's decline

- Reliance on single platform (Twitter) for distribution
- Subscription-only model limits hiring capacity

### Their Advice to Others

> Start a "little newsletter" with infrequent publishing as "defensive" strategy - "someday the choice might be made for you" regarding traditional employment
>
> Independent newsletter launching has become more challenging since 2020 due to:
>
> - Increased competition for subscription dollars
> - Reduced organic social distribution

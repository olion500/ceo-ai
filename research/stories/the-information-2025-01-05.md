# The Information Success Story

## The Story

### Problem Discovery

Jessica Lessin spent 8 years as a reporter for The Wall Street Journal covering Silicon Valley, breaking major deals and product launches. She was part of the team that was a 2012 Pulitzer Finalist for digital privacy coverage.

While at Harvard, she witnessed Facebook's earliest stages, shaping her future focus on tech. She identified that tech executives and investors needed **exclusive, in-depth coverage** they couldn't get elsewhere - and would pay premium prices for it.

**Key insight:** Premium subscription journalism could work if the content was truly differentiated and valuable enough to justify high prices.

### MVP Development

**Timeline:** December 2013

**Initial Features:**

- Subscription-only tech journalism
- No advertising model
- Focus on exclusive, investigative stories
- Premium pricing ($399/year)

**Tech Stack:**

- Custom publishing platform
- Email newsletter system
- Events platform

**Development Process:**

- Hired first employee (Eric Newcomer) in 2014
- Built advisory board (John Doerr, Jim VandeHei, Paul Steiger)
- Started small, focused on quality over scale

### Launch Strategy

**Platform:** Proprietary website (no Substack/Medium)

**Date:** December 2013

**Pre-launch:**

- Established reputation at WSJ
- Network of Silicon Valley sources
- Clear differentiation: no ads, premium only

**Launch Results:**

- Subscription-only from day one
- Premium pricing validated market need
- Profitable by 2016 (within 3 years)

### Growth Journey

#### Phase 1: Founding & First Employee (2013-2014)

- December 2013: Launched The Information
- 2014: Hired Eric Newcomer as first employee
- Formed advisory board with VCs and media veterans

#### Phase 2: Profitability & Expansion (2016-2017)

- 2016: Became profitable
- 2017: Expanded to Hong Kong and New York
- 22 staff members by 2017

#### Phase 3: Scale & Bundling (2020-2022)

- February 2020: Bloomberg Media partnership (joint bundle)
- 20,000+ subscribers milestone
- Launched proprietary data products

#### Phase 4: Premium Tier & Current (2023+)

- 2022: 45,000 paid subscribers
- 2023: Launched $999/year data service for C-suite
- 40+ employees across global bureaus

### Key Decisions

1. **No advertising, ever**
   - **Why:** Align incentives purely with readers
   - **Result:** Focus on subscriber value, not page views

2. **Premium pricing ($399/year)**
   - **Why:** Justify serious investigative journalism
   - **Result:** $1,000+ subscriber LTV

3. **Sole ownership**
   - **Why:** Editorial and business independence
   - **Result:** Lessin remains sole owner

4. **Geographic expansion**
   - **Why:** Cover tech globally
   - **Result:** Bureaus in Hong Kong, London, NY, DC, LA, Seattle

### Pivots & Changes

- **No advertising maintained:** Resisted temptation despite opportunities
- **Events added:** Commercial sponsorships for events (not content)
- **Data products:** $999/year tier for premium data (2023)

### Marketing Channels

**Primary Channel:** Exclusive Journalism

- Breaking stories that move markets
- Forbes reported: stories contributed to $97 billion in brokered industry deals
- Content so valuable professionals need it for their jobs

**Secondary Channels:**

- Networking events
- Industry reputation
- Word of mouth in tech/VC circles

### Revenue Model

**Pricing Structure:**

- Individual: $399/year
- C-suite data tier: $999/year (launched 2023)
- No advertising on content
- Events have commercial sponsorships

**Revenue Breakdown:**

- 45,000 subscribers × $399 = ~$18M ARR
- Plus events revenue
- Plus data products

**Key metric:** Subscriber LTV "way north of $1,000"

### Challenges & Solutions

1. **Challenge:** Temptation to add advertising
   - **Solution:** Stayed focused on subscription-only
   - **Result:** Maintained premium positioning

2. **Challenge:** Scaling quality journalism
   - **Solution:** Slow, careful hiring
   - **Result:** 40+ employees maintaining quality

3. **Challenge:** Competition from free content
   - **Solution:** Differentiated, exclusive content
   - **Result:** Profitable since 2016

## All Tactics & Strategies Used

### Tactic 1: Premium Pricing from Day One

- **What They Did:** Launched at $399/year (no free tier)
- **How They Implemented:** Subscription-only, no advertising
- **Context:** "You have to offer something that subscribers can't get anywhere else"
- **Results Achieved:** Profitable by 2016, 45K subscribers
- **Time Investment:** Ongoing high-quality journalism
- **Cost:** Significant editorial investment

### Tactic 2: Exclusive Breaking News

- **What They Did:** Focused on stories no one else could get
- **How They Implemented:** Deep Silicon Valley sources, investigative reporting
- **Context:** Differentiation through exclusivity
- **Results Achieved:** Contributed to $97 billion in industry deals
- **Time Investment:** Full editorial team
- **Cost:** Journalism salaries and resources

### Tactic 3: Advisory Board Leverage

- **What They Did:** Assembled high-profile advisory board
- **How They Implemented:** John Doerr (VC), Jim VandeHei (Politico), Paul Steiger (ProPublica)
- **Context:** Credibility and industry connections
- **Results Achieved:** Network effects, credibility boost
- **Time Investment:** Relationship building
- **Cost:** Advisory relationships

### Tactic 4: Strategic Partnerships

- **What They Did:** Bloomberg Media partnership for joint bundle
- **How They Implemented:** Joint subscription offering
- **Context:** Expand reach through established brand
- **Results Achieved:** Access to Bloomberg's audience
- **Time Investment:** Partnership negotiation
- **Cost:** Revenue sharing

### Tactic 5: Global Bureau Expansion

- **What They Did:** Opened bureaus in Hong Kong, London, NY, DC, LA, Seattle
- **How They Implemented:** Strategic hiring in key tech markets
- **Context:** Follow the story globally
- **Results Achieved:** Comprehensive coverage
- **Time Investment:** Years of expansion
- **Cost:** Significant operational investment
